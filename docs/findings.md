# Research Findings

## Study overview

The Family Orb prototype was evaluated in a single-session usability study with eight adults aged 60 or older at two senior centers in the northeastern United States.

The study combined structured signal-composition tasks, observation logs, and semi-structured interviews.

## What worked

- Participants broadly understood the core interaction.
- Participants were able to assign personal meanings to colors and light patterns.
- Steady signals were often associated with calm or routine updates.
- Fast flicker was more readily associated with urgency or strong emotion.
- Participants viewed Family Orb as a complement to phone communication rather than a replacement.

## Design challenges

### Slow flicker was ambiguous

Three participants did not identify a use case for the slow-flicker condition. This suggests that signal states should be perceptually distinct rather than differing only in intensity or speed.

### Error recovery needs improvement

Participants suggested:

- Cancel codes.
- Resend behavior.
- Clearer confirmation.
- A small display showing the selected state.
- A way to distinguish selected, sent, and received states.

### Meanings require coordination

Participants valued personalized signals but emphasized that senders and recipients must agree on their meanings in advance. A printed reference card or cheat sheet may help families maintain a shared vocabulary.

## Accessibility implications

Five participants spontaneously identified potential value for:

- Deaf and hard-of-hearing people.
- People with low vision.
- People with dexterity limitations.
- Nursing-home or hospital residents.
- Non-native English speakers.

These observations should be treated as design opportunities rather than evidence that the current prototype is already accessible to all of these groups.

## Design implications

Future iterations should:

- Avoid relying on color alone.
- Add haptic or multimodal feedback.
- Improve error recovery.
- Test a clearer set of signal patterns.
- Include participatory co-design with the communities who may use the system.
- Evaluate networked communication with remote family members.

## Scope and limitations

The current study was single-session and included eight participants. Signals were logged locally rather than transmitted to remote family members. The findings therefore describe early learnability, interpretation, and perceived use. More long-term adoption or real-world family communication outcomes can be validate in future study.
